\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The formula below maps an amino-acid sequence to the full equilibrium distribution of the radius of gyration $\Rg$ of
the chain in HPS1 coarse-grained molecular dynamics (298~K, 150~mM ionic strength). It uses the chain length $N$ and
four sequence descriptors: two branches of the sequence charge decoration, the mean hydropathy and the excess of a
contact-energy score over a length-dependent threshold. It has \val{cf.n_parameters} parameters (\val{cf.n_coefficients} coefficients and the two constants of the CE
threshold). It was fitted by maximum likelihood to the stored MD frames of \val{data.n_train} training proteins. On
\val{cf.val.n} held-out validation proteins it predicts $\langle\Rg\rangle$ with a mean absolute error of
\val{cf.val.mean_err_pct}\% and the standard deviation with \val{cf.val.std_err_pct}\%. The median Wasserstein-1
distance of the full distribution is \val{cf.val.w1_pct_median}\% of $\langle\Rg\rangle$. On the \val{cf.test.n} test
proteins, scored once for this form, the three errors are \val{cf.test.mean_err_pct}\%, \val{cf.test.std_err_pct}\% and
\val{cf.test.w1_pct_median}\%.
\end{abstract}

\section{The distribution}
With $s=\Rg/S_m$ the density is the two-exponent form of Victor and Lhuillier (J.\ Chem.\ Phys.\ 92, 1362 (1990),
Eqs.~(1), (2), (7), in $d=3$):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p}
p(\Rg)=\frac{1}{Z\,S_m}\exp\!\Big[-A\Big(\frac{s^{\delta}}{\delta}+\frac{s^{-g}}{g}\Big)\Big],\qquad
\delta=\frac{1}{1-\nu},\qquad g=\frac{3}{3\nu-1},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Z}
Z(A,\nu)=\int_0^\infty \exp\!\Big[-A\Big(\frac{s^{\delta}}{\delta}+\frac{s^{-g}}{g}\Big)\Big]\,ds .
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
\item $S_m$ is the most probable $\Rg$: $\partial_s\ln p=-A\,(s^{\delta-1}-s^{-g-1})$ vanishes at $s=1$.
\item $\nu\in(1/3,1)$ sets both tails. The compressed side falls as $\ln p\sim-s^{-g}$, the stretched side as
  $\ln p\sim-s^{\delta}$. For a good-solvent chain ($\nu=0.588$) $g=3.93$ and $\delta=2.43$; as $\nu\to1/3$ the
  compressed side becomes a vertical wall.
\item $A>0$ sets the width: the larger $A$, the narrower the distribution.
\end{itemize}
Moments follow from
\begin{equation}
\langle\Rg^k\rangle=S_m^k\,\frac{1}{Z}\int_0^\infty s^k\exp\!\Big[-A\Big(\frac{s^{\delta}}{\delta}+\frac{s^{-g}}{g}\Big)\Big]\,ds .
\end{equation}
$Z$ and the moments have no closed form for general $(A,\nu)$. They are evaluated by the trapezoidal rule in
$t=\ln s$ on $[-3,2]$ with 4{,}001 points (integrand $e^{t}\exp[-A(e^{\delta t}/\delta+e^{-gt}/g)]$). Normalised
this way, the density of every validation protein integrates to 1 within \val{cf.val.max_mass_dev} on an independent
grid.

\section{The parameters as functions of the sequence}
With $L=\ln N$:
\begin{align}
\ln S_m &= b_0+b_L L+b_{LL}L^2+\beta_+s_++\beta_hh+\sum_{x\in\{s_-,\,\lam\}}\big(\beta_x+\beta_{xL}\,L\big)\,x, \label{eq:Sm}\\
u &= \gamma_0+\gamma_+ s_+ +\gamma_{+N}\frac{s_+}{N}+\gamma_- s_- +\gamma_\lambda\lam+\gamma_h h,\qquad \nu=\frac13+\frac23\,\expit(u),\label{eq:nu}\\
\ln A &= a_0+a_\nu\,\nu+\frac{a_N}{N}+a_\lambda\lam .\label{eq:A}
\end{align}
$\expit(u)=1/(1+e^{-u})$ keeps $\nu$ inside $(1/3,1)$. The amplitude depends on the sequence through $\nu$, through
$1/N$ (the finite-length correction) and through $\lam$; it has no regression of its own. The term $s_+/N$ lets like-charge
repulsion raise $\nu$ more in short chains than in long ones. The coefficients are in Table~\ref{tab:coef}. With them inserted:
\begin{align*}
\ln S_m ={}& -1.73956+0.672772\,L-0.0063239\,L^2+0.192433\,s_+\\
 &+(-0.663359+0.243714\,L)\,s_- +(-0.00201655-0.0792066\,L)\,\lam-0.0871931\,h,\\[2pt]
u ={}& -0.284599+0.128824\,s_+ +\frac{23.5874\,s_+}{N}+1.70886\,s_--0.134595\,\lam-0.234043\,h,\\[2pt]
\ln A ={}& -0.09739+2.36289\,\nu+\frac{11.5194}{N}-0.323979\,\lam ,
\end{align*}
with $S_m$ in nm, and $h=\max(\mathrm{CE}+1.4318127-0.25\,L,\,0)$.

\paragraph{Parameter count.} $9+6+4=19$ coefficients plus the two constants of the CE threshold
$c(N)=c_0+c_1\ln N$, both set from the training data: $c_1=0.25$ was chosen from $\{-0.5,0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}$ by
three-fold cross-validation on the training proteins (Section~\ref{sec:threshold}), and $c_0$ is the median of
$\mathrm{CE}-c_1\ln N$ over the 3{,}223 training proteins: 21 parameters. The constants inside the descriptors
(Section~\ref{sec:desc}) were fixed before this fit and are not counted.

\paragraph{How this form was reached.} An earlier 24-parameter form had $\ln N$ in $u$, no $s_+/N$, the products
$s_+\ln N$ and $h\ln N$ in $\ln S_m$, and $s_+$ in $\ln A$; it capped $\nu$ for short, like-charged chains. The term
$s_+/N$ was added and those four terms removed one at a time, each change judged by the out-of-fold NLL (a removal may not
raise it by more than two standard errors, paired per protein with one sequence cluster as one unit; an addition must lower
it by more than two) and by the NLL on the length and contact-energy sets (not higher than the earlier form by more
than $10^{-3}$). The next removal, of $\gamma_+$, failed. Other shapes for the term ($s_+N^{-\Delta}$, saturation in $s_+$
or in $N$) and terms in $h/N$ did not pass. The choice was made after seeing training residuals of the earlier form and its
test score, so the gain over it is not a blind estimate.

\paragraph{Charge decoration branches $s_\pm$.} With the sequence charge decoration
$\mathrm{SCD}=\frac1N\sum_{i<j}q_iq_j\,(j-i)^{1/2}$,
\begin{equation}
s=\operatorname{asinh}\!\Big(\frac{5\,\mathrm{SCD}}{N}\Big),\qquad s_+=\max(s,0),\qquad s_-=\min(s,0).
\end{equation}
$s_+>0$ for a sequence whose like charges dominate (net-charge repulsion); $s_-<0$ for a sequence whose opposite
charges are segregated into blocks. Well-mixed sequences have $s\approx0$. Of the training proteins, \val{data.frac_scd_pos_pct}\% have
$s_+>0$ and \val{data.frac_scd_neg_pct}\% have $s_-<0$.

\paragraph{Mean hydropathy.} $\lam=\frac1N\sum_i\lambda_i$.

\paragraph{Contact energy CE and its excess $h$ over the CE threshold.} CE is a soft maximum (log-sum-exp) over pairs of chain segments of
their attraction, weighted by the probability that the loop between them closes. For each segment width $w\in\{5,\,9,\,15,\,w_N\}$, with
$w_N=\max(3,\operatorname{round}(N/8))$, and only if $2w\le N$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Window averages over residues $a,\dots,a+w-1$ (0-based, $a=0,\dots,N-w$): $\bar q_a$, $\bar\lambda_a$.
\item For every pair of non-overlapping windows, $b-a\ge w$, the pair energy
  $E_{ab}=w\,\big(e_0+\alpha\,\bar q_a\bar q_b+\beta\,(\bar\lambda_a+\bar\lambda_b)/2\big)$.
\item The Gaussian-chain weight of the contact: with window centres $x=(a+w/2)/N$, $y=(b+w/2)/N$, loop fraction
  $\ell=y-x$ and $\tau_\ell=1-\ell$,
  \[
  K_{ab}=1-6\Big[\frac{\ell^3}{12}+\frac{\tau_\ell^3}{6}+\frac{\ell\,\big(x^2+(1-y)^2\big)}{2}+\frac{\tau_\ell\,\ell^2}{6}\Big],
  \]
  the fractional drop of the mean-square radius of gyration of a Gaussian chain when positions $x$ and $y$ are joined
  (floored at $10^{-12}$).
\item $S_w=\ln\sum_{b-a\ge w}(b-a)^{-3/2}\,K_{ab}\,\exp(-E_{ab}/\tau)$, with $(b-a)^{-3/2}$ the Gaussian
  loop-closure factor.
\item $z_w=(S_w-\mu_w)/\sigma_w$, where $\mu_w$ and $\sigma_w$ are the mean and the sample standard deviation of $S_w$
  over the 3{,}223 training proteins that use width $w$ (all of them, except for $w=15$, which the 101 chains with
  $N<30$ do not use). They are fixed constants (values below), so CE is a function of the sequence alone.
\end{enumerate}
$\mathrm{CE}$ is the mean of $z_w$ over the widths used ($\mathrm{CE}=0$ if none qualifies, which happens only for $N<6$).
The formula uses only the part of CE above the CE threshold $c(N)$; below it, the contact energy has no effect:
\begin{equation}
h=\max\big(\mathrm{CE}-c(N),\;0\big),\qquad c(N)=c_0+c_1\ln N,\quad c_0=-1.4318127,\ c_1=0.25 .
\end{equation}
The CE threshold rises with chain length, from \val{cf.ce_threshold_at_20} at $N=20$ to \val{cf.ce_threshold_at_184}
at $N=184$ and \val{cf.ce_threshold_at_490} at $N=490$. CE itself grows with $N$, with a least-squares slope of
\val{data.ce_slope_lnN} per unit of $\ln N$ over the training proteins, and the threshold follows about 40\% of that
trend.
Constants: $e_0=-0.0187838$, $\alpha=0.453248$, $\beta=-0.176328$, the least-squares fit
$u_{ij}\approx e_0+\alpha q_iq_j+\beta(\lambda_i+\lambda_j)/2$ over the 210 residue pairs ($R^2=0.979$) of
$u_{ij}=B_{ij}/B^{\mathrm{rep}}_{ij}-1$, where $B_{ij}=-2\pi\int_0^\infty\big(e^{-V_{ij}(r)/k_BT}-1\big)\,dr$ for the HPS1
pair potential $V_{ij}$ at this salt and temperature and $B^{\mathrm{rep}}_{ij}$ is the same integral for its purely
repulsive part; $\tau=0.06$; and
$(\mu_w,\sigma_w)=(18.3490,\,5.97878)$, $(26.1023,\,7.61787)$, $(37.9729,\,10.1666)$, $(55.8291,\,31.5359)$ for
$w=5,\,9,\,15,\,w_N$. Full-precision values and the reference implementation are in \texttt{idprg/descriptors.py}.

\paragraph{Worked example: $\alpha$-synuclein ($N=\val{ex.asyn.N}$).}
\begin{itemize}
\item Descriptors: $\mathrm{SCD}=\val{ex.asyn.scd}$, so $s_+=0$ and $s_-=\val{ex.asyn.scd_neg}$;
  $\lam=\val{ex.asyn.mean_hydro}$; $\mathrm{CE}=\val{ex.asyn.ce}$ is below $c(140)=\val{ex.asyn.ce_threshold}$, so
  $h=0$.
\item Parameters: $\ln S_m=\val{ex.asyn.lnSm}$ ($S_m=\val{ex.asyn.Sm_nm}$~nm), $u=\val{ex.asyn.u}$,
  $\nu=\val{ex.asyn.nu}$ ($\delta=\val{ex.asyn.delta}$, $g=\val{ex.asyn.g}$) and $\ln A=\val{ex.asyn.lnA}$.
\item Distribution: $\langle\Rg\rangle=\val{ex.asyn.mean_nm}$~nm, standard deviation \val{ex.asyn.std_nm}~nm,
  skewness \val{ex.asyn.skew}.
\item The HPS1 simulation gives $\langle\Rg\rangle=\val{ex.asyn.hps1_mean_nm}$~nm.
\end{itemize}

\section{Fit}
\paragraph{Data.} HPS1 coarse-grained simulations of single chains at 298~K and 150~mM ionic strength (Debye screening
$\kappa=1.2738$~nm$^{-1}$), with 30{,}000 stored frames per protein. The proteins are split by MMseqs2 sequence clusters
(30\% minimum identity, 80\% coverage):
\begin{itemize}
\item \val{data.n_train} training proteins, used for the fit;
\item \val{data.n_val} validation proteins, used only for the scores below (one validation protein with corrupt stored
  frames is excluded);
\item \val{data.n_test} test proteins, scored once for this form, after its coefficients were fixed
  (earlier forms were scored on them too; \texttt{data/README.md}, test-split log).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Objective.} The mean over training proteins of the per-frame negative log-likelihood of all stored frames,
plus a ridge penalty on the regression slopes:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L=\frac1n\sum_{p=1}^{n}\Big[-\frac1{F}\sum_{f=1}^{F}\ln p\big(\Rg^{(p,f)}\big)\Big]
+\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}}{n}\,\big\|\boldsymbol\theta_{\text{slopes}}\big\|^2,\qquad n=3223,\ F=30{,}000,\ \lambda_{\mathrm r}=1 .
\end{equation}
The slopes of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Sm} and \eqref{eq:nu} enter the penalty on z-scored regressors; the intercepts and the
amplitude coefficients of Eq.~\eqref{eq:A} are not penalised. L-BFGS-B in double precision, started from a two-step
fit: ridge regressions of per-protein maximum-likelihood fits of Eq.~\eqref{eq:p} on the regressors, and least squares
for the amplitude law. It converges to $\mathcal L=\val{cf.objective_end}$.

\paragraph{CE threshold.}\label{sec:threshold} $c_0$ is set before the optimisation, as the median of
$\mathrm{CE}-c_1\ln N$ over the fit set. $c_1$ was chosen by the pooled out-of-fold NLL of the three-fold
cross-validation (Section~\ref{sec:perf}), under a rule fixed before the comparison: the best $c_1$ replaces the constant
threshold $c_1=0$ if its NLL is lower by more than two standard errors (paired per protein, with the proteins of one
sequence cluster as one unit) and its NLL on the length and contact-energy extrapolation sets is not higher by more
than $10^{-3}$.

\section{Performance}\label{sec:perf}
\paragraph{Metrics.} Per protein, against its MD ensemble; MD moments are taken over the full trajectory.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{NLL}: $-\frac1F\sum_f\ln p(\Rg^{(f)})$ over the 30{,}000 stored frames, $\Rg$ in nm (nats per frame),
  averaged over proteins. It grows with chain length, because longer chains have broader distributions in nm, so it is
  comparable only between models scored on the same proteins.
\item \textbf{$\langle\Rg\rangle$ error}: $|\langle\Rg\rangle_{\mathrm{pred}}/\langle\Rg\rangle_{\mathrm{MD}}-1|$;
  \textbf{bias}: the same without the absolute value. \textbf{$\sigma$ error}: the same for the standard deviation.
  \textbf{Skewness error}: $|\gamma_{\mathrm{pred}}-\gamma_{\mathrm{MD}}|$. All are averaged over proteins.
\item \textbf{$W_1$}: $\int|F_{\mathrm{pred}}(r)-F_{\mathrm{MD}}(r)|\,dr$, the Wasserstein-1 distance between the predicted
  CDF and the empirical CDF of the stored frames, in \% of $\langle\Rg\rangle_{\mathrm{MD}}$; median over proteins.
\item \textbf{Compact chains}: $\langle\Rg\rangle_{\mathrm{MD}}<0.8\,R_F(N)$, where
  $R_F=e^{\val{data.ln_a_F}}N^{\val{data.nu_F}}$~nm is the power law fitted to the training proteins'
  $\langle\Rg\rangle$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Test sets.} \emph{Validation} and \emph{test}: the formula above, scored on the \val{data.n_val}
validation and \val{data.n_test} test proteins.
\emph{Cross-validation}: the training proteins split into three folds by sequence cluster; all parameters except $c_1$
were refitted on two folds and scored on the third, and the out-of-fold scores were pooled (these are the scores from which
$c_1$ was chosen). \emph{Length} and \emph{contact-energy extrapolation}: all parameters except $c_1$ were refitted on a
restricted part of the training split and scored on training proteins outside it. Sequence-cluster mates of the scored proteins were removed from the fit set.
\begin{itemize}
\item Length: fit on $N\le200$ (\val{data.length.n_fit} proteins), scored on $N\ge300$ (\val{data.length.n_eval}).
\item Contact energy: fit on $\mathrm{CE}\le\val{data.ce_q75}$ (the training 75th percentile; \val{data.sticky.n_fit}
  proteins), scored on $\mathrm{CE}\ge\val{data.ce_q90}$ (the 90th percentile; \val{data.sticky.n_eval}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Domain of validity}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Conditions.} HPS1 at 298~K and 150~mM. The formula has no temperature or salt dependence.
\item \textbf{Descriptor ranges of the training proteins} (1st--99th percentile in brackets):
  \begin{itemize}
  \item $N=\val{data.range.N.min}$--\val{data.range.N.max};
  \item $s_+\in[0,\,\val{data.range.scd_pos.max}]$ ($\le\val{data.range.scd_pos.p99}$);
  \item $s_-\in[\val{data.range.scd_neg.min},\,0]$ ($\ge\val{data.range.scd_neg.p01}$);
  \item $\lam\in[\val{data.range.mean_hydro.min},\,\val{data.range.mean_hydro.max}]$
    ([\val{data.range.mean_hydro.p01}, \val{data.range.mean_hydro.p99}]);
  \item $\mathrm{CE}\in[\val{data.range.contact_energy.min},\,\val{data.range.contact_energy.max}]$
    ([\val{data.range.contact_energy.p01}, \val{data.range.contact_energy.p99}]).
  \end{itemize}
  Outside these ranges the formula extrapolates.
\item \textbf{Length.} Fitted on chains up to 200 residues, it predicts chains of 300--490 residues with a
  \val{cf.length.mean_err_pct}\% $\langle\Rg\rangle$ error and a \val{cf.length.std_err_pct}\% $\sigma$ error
  (Table~\ref{tab:perf}).
\item \textbf{Sticky chains.} Fitted on chains with $\mathrm{CE}\le\val{data.ce_q75}$, it overestimates the size of
  strongly sticky chains ($\mathrm{CE}\ge\val{data.ce_q90}$): $\langle\Rg\rangle$ error \val{cf.sticky.mean_err_pct}\%,
  bias \val{cf.sticky.bias_pct}\%, $\sigma$ error \val{cf.sticky.std_err_pct}\%. Beyond the contact-energy range it was
  fitted on, it should be expected to overestimate $\langle\Rg\rangle$.
\item \textbf{Compact chains} ($\langle\Rg\rangle_{\mathrm{MD}}<0.8\,R_F$; \val{cf.val.by.compact.n} of the
  \val{cf.val.n} validation proteins) are the least reliable regime. Their $\langle\Rg\rangle$ is overestimated by
  \val{cf.val.by.compact.bias_pct}\% on average, and $\sigma$ is off by \val{cf.val.by.compact.std_err_pct}\%. For the
  other validation proteins the errors are \val{cf.val.by.not_compact.mean_err_pct}\% ($\langle\Rg\rangle$, bias
  \val{cf.val.by.not_compact.bias_pct}\%) and \val{cf.val.by.not_compact.std_err_pct}\% ($\sigma$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Files}
In the \texttt{idprg} repository:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{model/closed\_form.json}: every coefficient and constant at full precision.
\item \texttt{idprg/closed\_form.py}: the formula (\texttt{parameters}) and its fit (\texttt{fit}).
\item \texttt{idprg/descriptors.py}: the descriptors.
\item \texttt{idprg/distribution.py}: the density, its moments and quantiles.
\item \texttt{idprg predict --closed-form SEQUENCE} prints $\langle\Rg\rangle$, $\sigma$, skewness, $\nu$ and $S_m$.
\item \texttt{idprg fit-closed-form} refits the formula; with \texttt{--fold 0}, 1 or 2 it gives the
  cross-validation fits, whose pooled scores are \texttt{results/closed\_form\_cv.csv}.
\item \texttt{idprg ablations} reruns Table~\ref{tab:abl}.
\item \texttt{results/closed\_form\_*.csv}: per protein, the predicted parameters, moments and scores.
\item \texttt{results/ce\_threshold\_cv.csv}: the choice of $c_1$. \texttt{results/ablations.csv}: the ablations.
\item \texttt{paper/numbers.tex}: every score quoted here, written by \texttt{paper/make\_numbers.py}.
\end{itemize}
