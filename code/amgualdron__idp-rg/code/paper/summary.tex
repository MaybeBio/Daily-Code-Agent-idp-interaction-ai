\section{What the model does}
An intrinsically disordered protein has no single structure. Its size is a distribution of the radius of gyration
$\Rg$ over the ensemble, and getting that distribution normally takes a molecular dynamics simulation. The model maps
the amino-acid sequence directly to the full equilibrium density $p(\Rg)$ that an HPS1 coarse-grained simulation at
298~K and 150~mM salt would produce. Mean, width, skewness and tails are all included.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A distribution with physical meaning.} The output is the two-exponent form of Victor and Lhuillier. It
  has three parameters: the most probable radius $S_m$, a width $A$, and an exponent $\nu$ that sets the asymmetry.
  It is fitted to individual MD frames by maximum likelihood.
\item \textbf{Two predictors.} The network is an MLP on nine sequence descriptors. The closed form has
  \val{cf.n_parameters} parameters and is linear in four descriptors and $\ln N$. Both are fitted to the same frames.
\item \textbf{A surrogate for HPS1, not a model of experiment.} The model reproduces the simulations. Against SAXS its
  error is that of the force field (Section~\ref{sec:bench}).
\end{itemize}

On the \val{data.n_val} validation proteins the network predicts $\mRg$ with a mean absolute error of
\pct{net.val.mean_err_pct} and the standard deviation $\sigma$ with \pct{net.val.std_err_pct}. The median
Wasserstein-1 distance between its distribution and the MD frames is \pct{net.val.w1_pct_median} of $\mRg$. On the
\val{data.n_test} test proteins, scored once, the errors are \pct{net.test.mean_err_pct}, \pct{net.test.std_err_pct}
and \pct{net.test.w1_pct_median}. The closed form is somewhat less accurate (Table~\ref{tab:scores}), most of all
on compact chains.

\section{Data}\label{sec:data}
\paragraph{Simulations.} Every protein was simulated with HPS1 (Dignon et al.\ 2018): one bead per residue,
Ashbaugh--Hatch short-range interactions with the residue hydropathies $\lambda$, and screened Coulomb interactions.
All runs use 298~K and 150~mM (Debye $\kappa=1.274$~nm$^{-1}$). 4{,}743 sequences were run for about $0.5N^{2.2}$
frames each. Per protein the data keep the moments of $\Rg$ over the full trajectory and a random subsample of
30{,}000 frames.

\paragraph{Cleaning.} A trajectory is kept if three conditions hold:
\begin{itemize}
\item the coefficient of variation of $\Rg$ is at most 0.5, which removes the 89 blow-ups;
\item the relative standard error of $\mRg$ is at most 3\%;
\item $N\ge20$.
\end{itemize}
One protein with corrupt stored frames is dropped, which leaves \val{data.n_proteins} proteins
(\texttt{data/README.md}).

\paragraph{Split.} MMseqs2 clusters at 30\% identity and 80\% coverage give \val{data.n_clusters} clusters. Whole
clusters go to one split:
\begin{itemize}
\item \val{data.n_train} training, \val{data.n_val} validation and \val{data.n_test} test proteins;
\item stratified by length tercile and the majority sign of the SCD;
\item every cluster holding more than 1\% of the proteins goes to training.
\end{itemize}
No validation or test protein shares a cluster with a training protein.

\paragraph{How each split was used.}
\begin{itemize}
\item All fitting used the training split only.
\item Model choices were made by cross-validation inside the training split, and on two extrapolation splits carved
  out of it (Section~\ref{sec:extrap}).
\item The validation split was used to compare final candidates.
\item The test split was scored once for the network, on 2026-10-27, and once for the closed form, on 2026-10-31, each
  after that model was fixed. It had also been opened for earlier versions, so it is not entirely unseen; the closed form
  was revised after the test score of its earlier 24-parameter version was known. It was never used to fit the released
  models (\texttt{data/README.md}, test-split log).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Experimental benchmark.} The benchmark has \val{data.n_benchmark} unique sequences ($N=\val{data.benchmark.N_min}$--\val{data.benchmark.N_max})
with a SAXS $\Rg$ and an HPS1 trajectory, in \val{data.n_benchmark_families} sequence families. It was not used for
any fitting or selection.

\section{The model}\label{sec:model}
\paragraph{Output.} With $s=\Rg/S_m$,
\begin{equation}
p(\Rg)\propto\exp\!\Big[-A\Big(\frac{s^{\delta}}{\delta}+\frac{s^{-g}}{g}\Big)\Big],\qquad
\delta=\frac1{1-\nu},\quad g=\frac3{3\nu-1},\quad \nu=\tfrac13+\tfrac23\operatorname{expit}(u).
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
\item $S_m$ is the mode of the distribution.
\item The exponent $\nu\in(1/3,1)$ sets both tails: $s^{-g}$ on the compressed side and $s^{\delta}$ on the stretched
  side.
\item $A$ sets the width.
\end{itemize}
The normaliser and the moments are computed by quadrature (\texttt{formulas.tex}, Section~1).

\paragraph{Inputs.} There are nine sequence descriptors, computed from the HPS1 residue charges and hydropathies:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\ln N$;
\item the two branches $s_\pm$ of the asinh-transformed sequence charge decoration;
\item the sequence hydropathy decoration SHD;
\item a loop-entropy-weighted contact energy CE of residue windows;
\item the fraction of charged residues;
\item the fractions of adjacent residue pairs that are both negative or both neutral;
\item the fraction of sticky residues.
\end{itemize}
The mean hydropathy $\lam$ enters only the amplitude law and the closed form. Definitions are in
\texttt{formulas.tex}, Section~2.

\paragraph{Network.}
\begin{equation}
\ln S_m=b_S+\nu_F\ln N+o_1,\qquad u=u_0+o_2,\qquad \ln A=\boldsymbol\kappa\cdot[1,\nu,\nu^2,1/N,\lam,s_+].
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathbf o$ is an MLP ($9\to\val{net.width}\to\val{net.width}\to2$, GELU) on the standardized inputs. Its last
  layer starts at zero, so training starts from the population average.
\item $\nu_F=\val{data.nu_F}$ is the Flory exponent of the training proteins' $\mRg$. $b_S$ and $u_0$ are fixed
  offsets.
\item The six amplitude-law coefficients $\boldsymbol\kappa$ are global and trained with the MLP.
\item In all there are \val{net.n_parameters} trained parameters.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Training.} The loss is the mean negative log-likelihood of the MD frames: 1{,}024 frames per protein,
redrawn every batch, and no other terms. The optimiser is AdamW (learning rate $10^{-3}$ with cosine decay, weight
decay $10^{-4}$, clip 1), with batch 64 and \val{net.epochs} epochs in float64. The seed is \val{net.seed}, and the
last epoch is kept. Retraining reproduces \texttt{model/network.eqx} bit for
bit (SHA-256 \texttt{\val{net.sha256}\dots}).

\paragraph{Closed form.} The same output distribution, with
\begin{itemize}
\item $\ln S_m$ linear in $s_+$, $s_-$, $\lam$, $h$ and $\ln N$, plus $s_-\ln N$, $\lam\ln N$ and $(\ln N)^2$.
\item $u$ linear in $s_+$, $s_+/N$, $s_-$, $\lam$ and $h$.
\item $\ln A$ on the law $[1,\nu,1/N,\lam]$.
\item $h=\max(\mathrm{CE}-c(N),0)$, the excess of the contact energy over the threshold
  $c(N)=\val{cf.c0}+\val{cf.c1}\ln N$.
\end{itemize}
These are \val{cf.n_coefficients} coefficients and two threshold constants. The closed form was fitted by maximum
likelihood on all frames of the training proteins, with a weak ridge penalty on the slopes. The slope $c_1$ was chosen
by three-fold cross-validation (Section~\ref{sec:choices}).

threshold ($c_1=0$) only if two conditions hold:
\begin{itemize}
\item its pooled out-of-fold NLL is lower by more than two cluster-paired standard errors;
\item it does not raise the extrapolation NLLs by more than $10^{-3}$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\paragraph{Metrics} (\texttt{formulas.tex}, Section~7). Each metric is computed per protein against its MD ensemble
and then averaged over proteins.
\begin{itemize}
\item NLL: per frame over all 30{,}000 stored frames, with the density in nm$^{-1}$. It grows with $N$, so it compares
  models only on the same proteins.
\item $\mRg$ and $\sigma$ errors: mean absolute relative errors against the full-trajectory moments. The bias is the
  signed mean.
\item Skewness error: the mean absolute difference.
\item $W_1$: the Wasserstein-1 distance to the stored frames, in \% of $\mRg$. The median is given, with the 90th
  percentile in brackets.
\item Compact chains: $\mRg_{\mathrm{MD}}<0.8\times$ the Flory baseline.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Validation and test}
\paragraph{Validation.} The network's median $\mRg$ error is \pct{net.val.mean_err_pct_median}, with the 90th
percentile at \pct{net.val.mean_err_pct_p90} and the maximum at \pct{net.val.mean_err_pct_max}.

\subsection{Extrapolation}\label{sec:extrap}
Each model was retrained, or refitted, on part of the training split and scored on training proteins outside it.
Cluster mates of the scored proteins were removed from the fit set.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Length:} fit on $N\le200$ (\val{data.length.n_fit} proteins) and scored on $N\ge300$
  (\val{data.length.n_eval}). The scored chains are up to \val{data.length.max_ratio}$\times$ longer than any fitted
  chain.
\item \textbf{Sticky:} fit on $\mathrm{CE}\le q_{75}$ (\val{data.sticky.n_fit}) and scored on $\mathrm{CE}\ge q_{90}$
  (\val{data.sticky.n_eval}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What the network ablations show.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Charge decoration $s_+$} matters most. Without it the validation $\mRg$ error rises from
  \pct{abl.net.val.reference.mean_err_pct} to \pct{abl.net.val.no_scd_pos.mean_err_pct}, and the length extrapolation
  error from \pct{abl.net.length.reference.mean_err_pct} to \pct{abl.net.length.no_scd_pos.mean_err_pct}.
\item \textbf{The contact energy} carries the sticky regime. Without it the sticky extrapolation error goes from
  \pct{abl.net.sticky.reference.mean_err_pct} to \pct{abl.net.sticky.no_contact_energy.mean_err_pct}.
\item \textbf{$s_-$, SHD and $\mathrm{patch}_-$}: leaving out any one of them raises the validation NLL and $\mRg$
  error, by less than for $s_+$ or CE but by several times the spread between seeds.
\item \textbf{FCR, $\mathrm{patch}_0$ and $f_{\mathrm{sticky}}$}: leaving out any one of them changes the scores by
  about the spread between seeds.
\item \textbf{Length alone} ($\ln N$ only) gives \pct{abl.net.val.ln_N_only.mean_err_pct}, so the sequence descriptors
  carry most of the accuracy.
\item \textbf{Architecture:} a wider or deeper network changes little; a narrower or shallower one is slightly worse.
\end{itemize}

\section{Domain of validity}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Conditions.} HPS1 at 298~K and 150~mM only. Nothing in the model depends on temperature or salt.
\item \textbf{Training ranges} (1st--99th percentile in brackets):
  \begin{itemize}
  \item $N=\val{data.range.N.min}$--\val{data.range.N.max} (\val{data.range.N.p01}--\val{data.range.N.p99});
  \item $s_+\le\val{data.range.scd_pos.max}$ ($\le\val{data.range.scd_pos.p99}$);
  \item $s_-\ge\val{data.range.scd_neg.min}$ ($\ge\val{data.range.scd_neg.p01}$);
  \item CE from \val{data.range.contact_energy.min} to \val{data.range.contact_energy.max}
    (\val{data.range.contact_energy.p01}--\val{data.range.contact_energy.p99});
  \item $\lam=\val{data.range.mean_hydro.min}$--\val{data.range.mean_hydro.max}
    (\val{data.range.mean_hydro.p01}--\val{data.range.mean_hydro.p99}).
  \end{itemize}
  \texttt{MODEL.md} lists all nine inputs.
\item \textbf{Confident:} chains inside these ranges, with $\mathrm{CE}<q_{90}$ and not compact. There the network's
  errors are at validation level.
\item \textbf{Long chains.} Moderate length extrapolation is supported (Section~\ref{sec:extrap}). Nothing beyond
  $N\approx500$ was tested.
\item \textbf{Sticky chains} ($\mathrm{CE}\ge q_{90}$) are the weak regime, and more so beyond the fitted range.
\item \textbf{Compact chains} ($\mRg_{\mathrm{MD}}<0.8\times$ Flory, about 5\%) are the least reliable. In validation
  their $\mRg$ bias is \pct{net.val.by.compact.bias_pct} for the network and \pct{cf.val.by.compact.bias_pct} for the
  closed form.
\end{itemize}
