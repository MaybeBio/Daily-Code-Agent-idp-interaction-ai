Intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs) and regions account for a substantial fraction of the human proteome and are involved in many disease-relevant processes. Yet their structural flexibility has long made them challenging targets, and they have received relatively little attention in binder design.

Binder design methods based on AlphaFold2, a protein structure predictor, typically start from a defined target structure. Disordered regions can adopt multiple conformations, so a single input structure captures only part of their conformational range. The Bindome, a database of protein binders designed \emph{in silico} using BindCraft, therefore focused on structured domains and contained binder candidates for 8,296 human proteins (40.9\% of the human reference proteome).

In this thesis, BindCraft was adapted for disordered targets to extend the coverage of the Bindome by prioritizing confidence at the predicted binding interface while allowing the target conformation to vary during design. The adapted workflow yielded 38,394 designs that passed the computational filters, with at least one accepted design for 493 of the 500 tested crops in the coverage analysis. Preprocessing identified 18,553 candidate crops across 9,464 proteins, including 4,654 proteins outside the original structured-domain target set. Together, the structured domains and disordered crops provided candidate target regions for 75.5\% of the human reference proteome, compared with 52.6\% for structured domains alone. Extending binder design to disordered regions provides a starting point for studying interactions that are difficult to perturb selectively.

Two collaborative applications further extended BindCraft on different target classes. Conformation-specific design examined whether binders could distinguish between different states of the same protein, while the ectodomain campaign prioritized binding sites away from potential glycosylation sites and shortlisted designs for 21 targets. Experimental validation of selected candidates was ongoing at the time of writing.
